Synth Riders for Nintendo Switch

A standalone flatscreen game built around a story campaign, released in December 2025. On a team of more than fifteen I owned three of its systems: character customization, the cutscene and dialogue engine, and the campaign.

System one

Character customization

Three riders to choose from, each with a wardrobe. Items are browsed by slot, bought with currency earned in play or unlocked through the campaign, previewed on the 3D model and kept between sessions, all with the controller.

How it works

  • Items are data. Each one records the slot it fills, the set it belongs to, how it unlocks and where it sorts. Its art loads on demand through Addressables.
  • Saved per rider through the game's standard save path, so loadouts work on console.
  • Controller-first interface. Tabs per slot, lists that scroll to follow the cursor, and price, lock and new markers on each item.
  • An editor tool builds new items, so adding content needs no code.

The hard parts

  • Swapping parts on a live skeleton. Changing legs or a head means binding a new skinned mesh to a skeleton that is already animating. The system keeps the bones on a hidden reference mesh and reassigns them to each part as it is created. The same path handles rigid accessories.
  • Characters froze after a swap. The animator stopped updating when new meshes appeared. Changing how it culls animation fixed it.
  • After launch, a fix that re-syncs campaign unlocks with the player's collection.
System two

Cutscene and dialogue engine

The story between songs: animatics with dialogue, music and sound, from the opening of the campaign to its ending. Lines advance with a button, and any scene can be skipped.

How it works

  • A timeline of events. Each event can show a video or a still image, play dialogue lines with a speaker name and colour, start or change music, trigger sound effects, and fade in or out.
  • Authored in the editor. A creation tool turns a new scene into data, not code.
  • Music that carries. A track can start on one event and continue through the next ones, with crossfades between tracks.
  • Localized dialogue, with the dialogue box placed per line.

The hard parts

  • Smooth playback on the console. Early versions flickered and dropped frames between events. Loading videos on demand, pooling render textures and capping the frame rate during playback removed both.
  • Safe skipping. Mashing the skip button used to lock a scene. Skipping now works at any point, including events with no dialogue.
System three

Campaign

A map of the story. Each node is a story beat, a song, or both. Clearing nodes opens new ones along branching paths, and milestones award new items for the riders.

How it works

  • A map laid out from data. Node positions for each branch are generated and the paths between them are drawn automatically, including several paths out of one node.
  • Flexible nodes. A node can launch a cutscene, a song, or a cutscene followed by a song.
  • Progress. Unlocking, cleared markers, a difficulty choice that persists between scenes, and the ending scene after the last node.

Where the systems meet

  • Rewards. Milestones award one or several items through the customization system, with an unlock screen that has its own animation and sound.
  • Story. Nodes hand off to the cutscene engine and take control back when a scene ends.
  • Feedback. Controller-first navigation, with a sound and a message when a node is still locked.
